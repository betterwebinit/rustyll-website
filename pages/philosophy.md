<section class="editorial-intro"><span class="eyebrow">HOW WE THINK ABOUT THE WEB</span><h2>Tools should make publishing feel clear.</h2><p>These principles are a direction for design and development. They give contributors a way to weigh tradeoffs as the project evolves.</p></section>
<ol class="principle-list">
  <li><span class="principle-number">01</span><div><h2>No magic</h2><p>Build steps and outcomes should be understandable. A command should do what it says, and errors should help you decide what to do next.</p></div></li>
  <li><span class="principle-number">02</span><div><h2>A useful starting point</h2><p>Defaults should help you begin with little configuration. When you need more control, the relevant settings should be easy to find.</p></div></li>
  <li><span class="principle-number">03</span><div><h2>Content comes first</h2><p>Writing, organizing and publishing content should remain the center of the workflow. Markdown and templates keep that work close to your files.</p></div></li>
  <li><span class="principle-number">04</span><div><h2>Stability matters</h2><p>Changes should consider existing sites. When a breaking change is necessary, provide a clear migration path and explain the reason.</p></div></li>
  <li><span class="principle-number">05</span><div><h2>Room to extend</h2><p>Keep common tasks approachable while allowing additional functionality through documented extension points.</p></div></li>
</ol>
